# 05 — Business, Awards & Sustainability: The Non-Robot Deliverables

**Scope:** everything a championship team produces that is *not* the robot — awards submissions, documentation, open-source build publishing, budget/sponsorship/grants, outreach, and team structure — plus FIRST's current official position on AI-generated content in award materials.

**Season context:** *BIOCORE presented by Haas*, part of the **FIRST CANOPY** season. Kickoff **January 9, 2027, 12:00 p.m. ET**. Sources: [BIOCORE Game & Season](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/frc/game-and-season), [2026-2027 FIRST CANOPY](https://www.firstinspires.org/first-canopy).

**Research date:** 2026-08-21. All figures and deadlines below were pulled from live official sources on that date. Documents cited are the versions current as of that date (Judge Manual Rev 2 – 2/18/2026; Submitted Awards page updated 7/29/2026; Fundraising Guide Rev. July 2026; Team Organization Best Practices Rev. Aug. 2026).

> **The single most important finding for a small, underfunded team** is in FIRST's own Impact Award judging instructions to judges: *"Consider utilization of team resources, especially when comparing large teams vs small teams and focus on how teams sustain their activities. In short, what did they accomplish with the resources available to them?"* — [FIRST Impact Award Judging Guidelines](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-judging-guidelines.pdf), Rev. Jan 2026. The rubric is explicitly resource-normalized. You are not competing on absolute outreach volume.

## 1. Awards Landscape

### 1.1 The three "Cultural Awards" — the only judged awards that advance you

Per the [2026 Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf) (Ch. 4): *"Cultural Awards are the awards that have the most impact on the community and may qualify the team to move on to the District Championship or FIRST Championship. The three Cultural Awards are the FIRST Impact Award (FIA), Engineering Inspiration (EI), and Rookie All Star (RAS)."*

| Award | Advances? | Submitted or judged on-site? | Rookie-eligible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FIRST Impact Award** (formerly Chairman's) | Yes — Regional/DCMP winner → FIRST Championship | Pre-submitted essay + exec summaries, then on-site interview | **No.** First-year teams explicitly ineligible and will not be interviewed |
| **Engineering Inspiration Award** (sponsored by SpaceX) | Yes | Judged from pit/on-site interviews — **no pre-submission** | Yes |
| **Rookie All-Star Award** | Yes | Judged on-site — no pre-submission | Only team numbers **10,900+** |

**Cultural award restriction (important):** *"Once a team has won a cultural award, they may not win that award again at the same level of competition."* Winning FIA at your first event means you cannot win FIA again at another Regional/District event that season — but you *can* still win EI or any other award there. District teams are not eligible for any cultural award at a Regional or a different district's event. (Judge Manual, Ch. 4.)

**At District Championships**, only teams that won EI or RAS at a district event are judged for those awards at DCMP. (Judge Manual, "District Championship (DCMP) Events".)

See [Championship Eligibility](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/championship-eligibility) for the current-season slot math.

### 1.4 Two under-contested awards a small team should target deliberately

**Rising All-Star Award** — new, and structurally favorable. The Judge Manual describes it as recognizing a team that has persisted through challenges despite being young, whether it is a new team or one with recent turnover in membership. Critically, *"This award can be given to a team with any team number"*: a team in its second, third or twentieth year that has grown significantly or succeeded despite challenges qualifies, and judges are told to ask every team about its "newness." A veteran small team that has just rebuilt its roster, changed coaches, moved facilities, or overhauled its processes is squarely in scope. It is an *optional* award, and FIRST tells judges that not awarding it *"should be a rare event."*

**Team Sustainability Award (Dow)** — the criteria are literally your business operations. From the [Award Workbook](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/award-workbook.pdf): the team must explain plans and actions across **People** (recruit/train/retain students, mentors, sponsors), **Prosperity** (how the team fundraises, creates and tracks budgets, identifies and manages risk), and **Planet** (environmental impact assessment and mitigation, and how that strategy affects team longevity). If you build the budget model and sponsor tracker described in §6, you have most of this award's evidence as a byproduct.

### 1.5 "Spread the Wealth" — a real strategic constraint

Judge Advisors are instructed to enforce equitable distribution: *"Do not award the same team more than 1 judged award at a single event."* Importantly, this is **within** an event only: *"judges should not use the fact that a team won award X at an earlier event to exclude them from award X at the current event"* (cultural awards excepted). Also: *"do not spread the wealth between judged awards and field awards"* — a team winning the event can still take a judged award. (Judge Manual, "Equitable Award Distribution".)

**Implication:** targeting three machine awards at one event is wasted effort — you can only win one. Pick a primary and a backup, and let the rest fall where they may.

### 1.6 What judges do on-site

- **Pit interviews.** Judges circulate the pits; there is no scheduled slot. *"DO NOT skip a team just because they are not in their pit"* — but FIRST's team-facing guidance is to *"Always keep someone in your Pit that can speak to the judges -OR- have some type of sign telling judges where they can find your team."* ([Best Practices for Teams](https://community.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/best-practices-for-teams.pdf), Rev. Feb 2026.)
- **Match Observer judges.** At least 2 judges per event are designated MOs who watch matches and cross-check what teams claim in the pits. *"Pit judges should validate what teams tell them with the Match Observers."* Do not oversell your autonomous in the pit if it does not run on the field.
- **Judges will not talk to mentors.** *"Judges will talk to the students, not to the mentors from the team."*
- **Pit handouts:** *"There are no restrictions on page limits of documents"* for pit materials — but judges are not expected to review everything, and *"Materials are not typically returned to teams so teams should not give them the only copy."* FIRST recommends a **one-page summary** of key machine attributes or team attributes, with a picture of the robot.

## 2. The Impact Award in Detail

### 2.1 Exact format and limits

- **13 executive summary questions**, each **500 characters including spaces and punctuation**.
- **1 optional 14th question**: 250 characters, where you ask *your* judges one question that they must answer in your post-event feedback.
- **Essay: 10,000 characters including spaces and punctuation.**
- Total prose budget: roughly **16,750 characters** (~2,600–2,800 words). This is a small, dense document, not a report.

**The 13 executive summary questions** (verbatim topics, from the Submitted Awards page):

1. Impact of the FIRST program on team participants within the last 3 years (graduation %, college, STEM careers, leadership, mentors/sponsors).
2. Describe your community and its unique opportunities and circumstances (geography, diversity, language, socioeconomic, cultural expectations).
3. Team's methods, emphasis past 3 years, for spreading the FIRST Mission — **effective, scalable, sustainable, creative**.
4. Team's goals and progress toward fulfilling FIRST's Vision.
5. What impact have you seen from those efforts? **How does your team measure impact?**
6. Specific examples of team/members acting as role models for the FIRST community; how do you share best practices with other teams?
7. Initiatives to **Mentor and/or Start** other FIRST teams.
8. Other initiatives created, grown, sustained, or participated in (FIRST or otherwise) to inspire young people; what outcomes?
9. Partnerships and relationships with other organizations (teams, sponsors, educational institutions, government, philanthropic).
10. Efforts to promote **STEM for Everyone™** within your team, FIRST, and communities.
11. How you ensure your team and your initiatives will be **sustainable**.
12. **One area in which your team needs to improve**, and steps actively being taken.
13. Other matters of interest — unique, noteworthy, or high-impact aspects.

Note questions 5, 11 and 12 are self-assessment questions. Question 12 in particular is a trap for teams that write a puff piece; FIRST asks for a genuine weakness plus active remediation.

### 2.2 What the essay is for (FIRST's own guidance)

> *"The essay should avoid merely duplicating information provided in the executive summary questions."* … *"The most effective essays are characterized by an overview of team activities during the last 3 years, followed by in-depth discussion of notable activities during the most recent 12-18 months."*

FIRST also explicitly says: *"Teams are encouraged to use some of this space to explain how their team is structured and the number of mentors and students on the team."* For a small team, **state your size**. The judging rubric is resource-normalized, and judges cannot normalize for a headcount you never gave them.

### 2.3 Structural characteristics of actual winning submissions

FIRST publishes every winning submission. The 2026 index is [2026-fia-winners.pdf](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-essays/2026/2026-fia-winners.pdf), which links roughly 80 winning essays plus videos and presentations. **The essay PDFs follow a stable, predictable URL pattern**, which makes the whole corpus trivially harvestable for study:

```
https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-essays/<YEAR>/<TEAMNUMBER>.pdf
```

Verified examples: [1678 (Citrus Circuits, 2026)](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-essays/2026/1678.pdf), [9449 (Yellowjackets, Calgary, 2026)](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-essays/2026/9449.pdf). Prior years are indexed from the [FIA Winners page](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-winners) back to 2017. Championship-level winners and their videos/presentations are on the [Hall of Fame page](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/hall-of-fame) and [firsthalloffame.org](https://www.firsthalloffame.org/meet-the-teams/).

**Quantitative analysis of four 2026 winning submissions** (1678, 498, 7028, 9449), measured directly from the published PDFs:

| Metric | Finding |
|---|---|
| Essay length | **9,551 – 9,990 characters** of the 10,000 allowed (95.5%–99.9% utilization) |
| Executive summary block | ~8,300–8,800 characters including the printed question text |
| Numeric claim density | **2.9 – 10.4 numbers per 1,000 characters** |
| Percentage claims | 2–14 per submission |

Two takeaways. First, **every winner uses essentially the entire character budget** — an 8,000-character essay is leaving 20% of your argument on the table. Second, claim density splits sharply: the two large, well-resourced programs in the sample ran ~10 numbers per 1,000 characters, while the two smaller programs ran ~3. Both won. **Quantification density is not what separates winners** — but every claim that *is* made is specific.

**Structural pattern observed** (analysis, not reproduction): winning essays open with a controlling metaphor or thesis, declare 3–5 named program pillars as explicit section headers, and then fill each pillar with named programs carrying a number and a time-bounded outcome. 1678's 2026 essay, for example, organizes around four capitalized pillars and attaches to each a named program, a count, and a growth delta (e.g., a program expanding from 5 to 9 schools, an enrollment figure over a stated 3-year window). The recurring unit is **[named program] + [specific number] + [timeframe] + [what changed]**. Generic verbs ("we inspire our community") do not appear without an attached instance.

**Do not copy a winning essay's structure verbatim.** Judges read dozens of submissions and pre-read them before the event; formulaic mimicry reads as such. Use the corpus to calibrate density and specificity, not to template.

### 2.4 Video specification

Optional, but FIRST plays the winner's video at the awards ceremony, and its absence is noticed at higher levels.

- **16:9 widescreen.**
- **Not more than 3 minutes; not shorter than 1 minute.**
- Clean audio track; free of pops and hisses.
- **Free of copyright restrictions, including music.**
- Closed captions in English encouraged.
- **Do not identify minors by full name — first names only.**
- Acceptable forms: video footage, voice-over/music over stills, animated presentation, or a PowerPoint converted to video format.
- **Must be hosted on Dropbox, Box, or Google Drive**, public, and configured **to allow downloads**. YouTube/Vimeo links are not accepted.
- The URL cannot be edited after submission, but **the file behind it can be updated**. FIRST explicitly suggests submitting a Google Drive *folder* link so you can keep iterating between events — with the caveat that there must be exactly one file in the folder at event time.
- Requires agreement to the [Video Consent & Release of Rights Form](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-video-consent.pdf) in the portal.

**Small-team read:** a 90-second video assembled from stills with a clean voice-over meets spec fully and costs a weekend. The "PowerPoint converted to video" allowance is an explicitly sanctioned low-effort path.

### 2.5 The judged interview

- **12 minutes total, hard cap.** Up to **7 minutes** for the team's presentation **including setup**, and the remaining **5 minutes** for judge-led Q&A. If you present short, judges reclaim the time for Q&A.
- **Maximum 3 pre-college students** may present or answer.
- **One adult mentor may attend as a silent observer** — FIRST *"highly encourage[s]"* this so the mentor can give the team feedback afterward. The mentor may not assist, and may not record video/audio or take photos. Teams are **not** marked down for a mentor being present.
- Translator/interpreter allowed; adds up to 5 minutes (2 presentation + 3 Q&A).
- Judges **pre-read your submission** before the event and meet to compare questions beforehand. Your interview is answering a document they already have, not introducing yourself.

**Leave-behind materials:** *"strongly recommended that the materials are no larger than one 2" 3-ring binder (or equivalent volume if spiral wound)... in a single bound unit, and not as multiple handouts. The binder should focus on the team's activities going back no more than 3 seasons. Please do not try to document your team's entire history."*

### 2.6 Feedback loop — use it

Every team that interviews receives written feedback within **48 hours** of the event via the Dashboard and email. It answers exactly three things: (1) an area to improve, (2) something that impressed the judges, and (3) **your own submitted question**. Each is ≤500 characters.

FIRST publishes suggested questions: *"Is there something more that we can do to improve our presentation?"*, *"Is there anything missing from our submitted materials?"*, *"Which outreach activity do you feel we execute most effectively?"* Questions asking what is required to win — or inappropriate questions — are answered with *"Did not follow guidelines."*

Because feedback arrives within 48 hours and the submission itself is locked, **the deliverable you can actually iterate between events is the presentation and the video.** Plan for that.

### 2.7 A published end-to-end example worth studying

**Team 321 (RoboLancers, Philadelphia)** published their complete 2023 Championship-winning Impact package at [robolancers.com/about/awards/impact-award-2023](https://robolancers.com/about/awards/impact-award-2023/) — including an **annotated essay** explaining the narrative intent behind each section, an **annotated presentation script** with notes on word choice, pacing and intonation, a long and a short judging packet (5.5" × 8.5" booklets), their documentation binder approach, the video, and notes from a public Q&A session. This is the single most useful published artifact for learning the craft, because the annotations expose the reasoning rather than just the output.

## 3. Engineering Notebook / Design Documentation

### 3.1 The direct answer: FRC does not have an engineering notebook requirement

Unlike *FIRST* Tech Challenge — where the Engineering Portfolio/Notebook is a formally scored, required artifact — **FRC has no required notebook, no page limit, no rubric, and no award that is scored against a notebook.** Nothing in the [2026 Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf) or the [Award Workbook](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/award-workbook.pdf) requires, references, or scores a notebook.

**Do not port an FTC notebook workflow into FRC.** It is a large, ongoing labor cost with zero direct award return.

### 3.2 What FRC judges actually expect instead: *verbal traceability*

The machine awards are won by **being able to describe your process out loud in the pit**. Read the Award Workbook criteria literally — the recurring verb is "describe" and the recurring noun is "trace":

- **Excellence in Engineering:** *"A team must be able to describe... the engineering process they went through and can trace elements of the designs from conception through completion."*
- **Creativity Award:** *"A team must be able to describe the creative/unique feature(s) and can trace its conception and design"* — and describe how they mitigated the risk of failure.
- **Innovation in Control:** *"A team must be able to describe their controls innovation and can trace its conception, design, manufacturing..."*
- **Autonomous:** must describe how the robot perceives, navigates, positions and executes; **and** *"the factors the teams considered that could interfere with success"*; **and** the design, development and testing done. *"Consistent and reliable operation is weighted more heavily than the ability to score maximum points."*
- **Industrial Design:** must describe how the machine design is *"elegant, efficient (simple/executable), and practical"* — and it must be the *entire machine*, not one component.

**So the real deliverable is not a notebook. It is a small set of students who can narrate a decision history under questioning, backed by artifacts that jog memory and prove claims.** The documentation exists to support the conversation, not to be graded.

### 3.3 What elite teams keep anyway, and why

| Artifact | Purpose | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical / design binder** | Left in pit; supports pit-judge conversations and pit-scouting by other teams. Typical sections: design requirements & game analysis, strategy, subsystem one-pagers with CAD renders, wiring/electrical diagram, controls architecture, test data, iteration log | Moderate; assembled late in build |
| **One-page robot summary** | FIRST's own explicit recommendation for pit handouts — key machine attributes, novel components, controls approach, and a robot photo | Low — highest ROI item on this list |
| **Impact documentation binder** | Backs Impact claims. Judges use it to **verify** shortlist claims (see §7.2). Letters, attendance lists, hour logs, news clippings | Ongoing, low-per-event |
| **Open Alliance build thread** | Public, timestamped, chronological design record that *doubles* as the design history (see §4) | Low marginal — you are writing it anyway |
| **Public CAD + GitHub** | Proof of process; also the thing that makes you credible to other teams | Near-zero marginal if you already use Onshape/GitHub |

**The small-team play:** an Open Alliance build thread **is** your engineering notebook. It is chronological, timestamped, public, forces weekly reflection, and — unlike a private notebook — generates external feedback and reputation while you write it. You then print/curate the highlights into a thin pit binder and a one-page summary. One artifact, three uses.

### 3.4 Where the binder limits differ (a real gotcha)

The two limits are different and teams routinely confuse them:

- **Impact Award interview room:** strongly recommended **≤ one 2" 3-ring binder**, single bound unit, **≤3 seasons** of activity.
- **Pit (machine awards):** *"There are no restrictions on page limits of documents"* — but judges are not expected to read it all, and materials are typically **not returned**.

## 4. The Open Alliance

### 4.1 What it is

The FRC Open Alliance is a community organization — not a FIRST program — under which teams publicly publish their build season in real time: build thread, CAD, code, media. From [theopenalliance.org/frc](https://theopenalliance.org/frc):

> *"The FRC Open Alliance is an organization created to help FIRST® Robotics Competition teams easily share their information and statistics to a wider audience. The Alliance is a great resource for newer teams that are looking to get started or veteran teams that are looking to expand their capabilities. Being a member of an FRC Open Alliance team is not required to access the information of Alliance teams."*

**History:** founded **2020** by a group of teams reacting to the end of "The Bag" (the robot-sealing rule) and the risk that FRC would become more secretive. Started with **6 teams** on Chief Delphi; opened to all teams in 2022; now a joint FRC/FTC platform with nonprofit status (fiscally sponsored by The Hack Foundation / Hack Club, EIN 81-2908499) and industry partners.

### 4.2 Participation guidelines (verbatim, from the [official info repo](https://github.com/OpenAllianceFRC/info))

1. **Don't keep secrets** — *"Sharing and learning is our competitive advantage, not secrets. Don't withhold 'that one detail' that makes an idea or mechanism work. If you share it, share it all."*
2. **Try to update 1 or more times per week** — include analysis and notes, and thoughts on what went well and what did not.
3. **Use a platform conducive to asking and answering questions** — official update posts in a Chief Delphi build thread, cross-posted to Discord.
4. **Post lots of pictures and videos.**
5. **Open CAD and code are encouraged but not required.**
6. **Include the bad with the good** — *"We want a complete picture, not a highlight reel. Posting the bad helps everyone learn."*

Posts are tagged `#openalliance` and `#frc[teamNumber]`. Threads live in the [FRC Open Alliance category on Chief Delphi](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/c/first/open-alliance/89).

### 4.3 Scale and who participates

The [2026 FRC Open Alliance Information and Directory](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/2026-frc-open-alliance-information-and-directory/508112) thread listed **56 teams** in its directory as of its March 2, 2026 update. (The directory is opt-in — the Open Alliance *category* on Chief Delphi carries more threads than the directory lists, so 56 is a floor, not a census.)

The 2026 directory spans the full range of program maturity — from long-established programs (157, 340, 360, 509, 525, 900, 1540, 1730, 3061) through mid-tier teams (2656, 2826, 2869, 3161, 3284, 3467, 3749, 4328) to recent numbers (8626, 9280, 9642, 9644, 11025). **This is not an elite-only club**, which matters for a small team deciding whether it "belongs."

Format note: thread titles follow no strict requirement, but the conventional form is `FRC <number> <Team Name> | 2027 Build Thread | Open Alliance`. Threads should be made **wikis** (wrench icon → Make Wiki) so the top post stays editable as an index. There is also an **Open Alliance Show** — video segments recorded during weeks 2–6 of build season that teams sign up for, which per the organizers *"have the possibility to generate several thousand views showcasing your team."*

### 4.4 Why this is disproportionately valuable to a *small* team

1. **Free senior engineering review.** A public thread attracts unsolicited critique from experienced mentors across hundreds of teams. A team with two mentors buys design review it cannot otherwise staff. This is the single largest practical benefit.
2. **It generates Impact Award evidence that FIRST explicitly recognizes.** The [FIA Definitions](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-definitions.pdf) define **Published Resources** as resources created to aid teams with technical or non-technical issues that *"have been published or presented publicly."* A build thread, public CAD, and a public GitHub repo qualify directly. FIRST's caveat: *"Any team can publish resources, and many do, but how much actually gets used is what is more important"* — so **report the Reach** (view counts, thread views, GitHub stars/forks, downloads).
3. **Credibility with sponsors and judges.** A public, timestamped record of a season is unusually persuasive evidence of process for a team that cannot point to a trophy case.
4. **It is the cheapest possible documentation strategy.** See §3.3.
5. **Recruitment and alliance selection.** Being legible to other teams changes how you are picked and who helps you at events.

**Honest cost:** roughly 1–3 hours per week during build season for a student to write the update, plus the discipline to post failures. The "include the bad with the good" guideline is the part teams struggle with, and it is also the part that generates the most useful replies.

### 5.3 Realistic budget tiers

### 5.6 Sponsorship mechanics

**Tier design** — FIRST's guidance from the [Fundraising Guide](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/fundraising-guide.pdf):

- **Create 3–5 tiers.** FIRST's worked example: `$100–$499 / $500–$999 / $1,000–$1,999 / $2,000+`.
- Reserve the most appealing benefits (name on the robot) for the top tier.
- *"Don't promise to give a 10-foot cardboard cutout of your robot if you can't follow through."*
- Common benefits: logos on apparel/website/robot/social (with links and tags), student thank-you cards, robot demos at the sponsor's facility, competition tours.

**Where sponsors come from:** the highest-yield channel is *"a list of parent, grandparent, mentor, and student employers. There are many companies that are familiar with FIRST and have funds set aside for sponsorship if a team has an active mentor or parent who is an employee."* Corporate matching and employee-directed giving programs are the small-team unlock.

**In-kind is undervalued.** FIRST's list: screen printing for apparel; laser cutting, waterjet, or sheet metal services; hand tool sets and fastener stock; **leftover blank stock (tubing, sheet metal) from local manufacturing shops**; team meals; hotel discounts; bookkeeping services. For a T0/T1 team, converting $4,000 of the robot budget into in-kind machining and stock is often easier than raising $4,000 cash.

**Retention beats acquisition.** *"It's more efficient and effective to keep an existing sponsor year after year than find a new one."* Three actions FIRST names: track what each sponsor gave each year; report back the specific impact of their money; maintain year-round contact (short email updates, invitations to outreach events, an open house).

**How to receive money** — the four routes, with real tradeoffs:

| Route | Advantage | Challenge |
|---|---|---|
| School district account | District handles accounting and compliance | Slow, cumbersome to actually spend |
| **FIRST Dashboard** | Uses FIRST's 501(c)(3); zero admin overhead | **Restricted funds can only pay FIRST expenses (registration)**; unrestricted funds require a regrant process |
| Booster club | Independent 501(c)(3) | You now run a nonprofit: filings, compliance, board |
| **Fiscal sponsor** (4-H, Boys & Girls Clubs, **Hack Club Bank**) | 501(c)(3) status without running one; sponsor handles compliance | Must follow their rules; may charge a fee |

For a small team without an existing booster club, **fiscal sponsorship is usually the right answer** — it gives donors tax deductibility (which FIRST notes *"confers legitimacy on your donation request"*) without adding a nonprofit-administration workload you cannot staff. Note that the Open Alliance itself uses Hack Club as its fiscal sponsor.

**Templates** (all verified live) on the [Team Management Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/team-management-resources) page:

## 6. Outreach and Sustainability: What Counts

### 6.1 FIRST has formal definitions and they are binding

This is the part most teams get wrong. FIRST publishes the [FIRST Impact Award Definitions](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-definitions.pdf) (Rev. Jan 2026) and states: *"All teams are required to adhere to the following Definitions in their submission and interview."* Judges are told the definitions are the bolded/italicized terms in their guidelines, and that *"Teams are responsible for policing their own choice of words."*

**Started** — all of the following:
1. Helped the team form & ensure sustainability (organizing, managing, training students/mentors, recruiting) **OR** funded/sourced funding for **≥50% of the team registration fee**; **AND**
2. The Started team **agrees** you Started them; **AND**
3. The Started team **competes in an official FIRST event during the season**; **AND**
4. You provide guidance and support throughout their first season.

**Mentored** — both of:
1. **Consistent** communication (in person, phone, email, video) on technical or non-technical FIRST-specific issues — *"a general standard"* of roughly weekly, with discretion for edge cases;
2. The Mentored team **agrees** you Mentored them.

FIRST lists explicit non-examples: answering a single email; letting a team use your machinery; hosting them during bad weather; giving them a part; letting them use your practice field. Those are *"assisting,"* not Mentoring.

**Published Resources** — both of: (1) you created resources to aid teams with technical or non-technical FIRST issues, and (2) they were published or presented publicly. FIRST's caution: *"Any team can publish resources, and many do, but how much actually gets used is what is more important"* — **report Reach**.

**Host** an event — all of: team members do the majority of planning; team members do the majority of on-site execution (or recruit and supervise the volunteers who do); the team holds **primary accountability**; both mentors and students involved. *"Hosting an event essentially means that this event would not be possible without the efforts and actions of the given team."* Providing the most volunteers does **not** make you the Host.

**Supported** an event — any of: multiple team members involved in some planning, **or** multiple team members volunteering for the **entirety** of the event. One member or one mentor does not qualify.

**Reached** — *"the quantity of people that have observed or interacted with the team... Reach requires tangible metrics or interaction with the team, not merely seeing the team in the background."* FIRST's worked examples are unusually strict and worth internalizing:

- 6,000,000 watch a TV show **featuring** your robots → Reached 6,000,000.
- 6,000,000 watch a show where your robots are **background props** → Reached **0**.
- 1,000,000 attend an event, **500 see your exhibit** → Reached **500**, not 1,000,000.
- 30,000 at a football game where you **perform at halftime** → Reached 30,000.
- 30,000 at a game where your **name is on the big screen** → Reached **0** ("not a tangible interaction").
- 700 Instagram followers → Reached 700. But per-post: *"Social Media post to 1,000 followers, with 500 who engaged with it (liked, forward, share, etc.), has reached 500 people."*

And the integrity clause: *"it is important that teams do not embellish or exaggerate these numbers, as doing so would paint a misleading picture... When in doubt, teams should try and estimate on the low end."*

**Advocated** — meeting with or serving as a resource for government officials, community leaders, school administration, or business leaders to promote STEM/FIRST policy change. **Non-examples:** tweeting at officials; volunteering for a campaign; **tabling or handing out flyers** (*"people have to engage not just a passive act"*); advocating at your school for your own team's benefit.

### 6.2 How claims get verified

From the [Judging Guidelines](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-judging-guidelines.pdf): *"After Judges deliberate and have their shortlist of top contenders, Judges can use the FIRST Impact Award Documentation Form to verify team's claims."* And from the Submitted Awards page: *"As a team be sure to review the submission and recognize that judges may look to verify claims."*

The [Documentation Form](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-documentation-form.pdf) ([editable Word version](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-documentation-form.docx)) is a simple table: activity/team + location, date, **which official Definition it falls under**, type of documentation, and a Documentation ID (`ID-001`, …). You attach the evidence in ID order and submit a public link to the whole package.

**It is optional but strategically near-mandatory:** *"providing it shows the Judges that your activities are well planned and documented,"* and *"it does help show to the judges that they are using the correct terminology and offers proof of the activities."* Only activities within the **past 3 years** should be included. Finalize it **one week before each event** so judges have time to review.

**Populating it is a pure clerical task** — which makes it one of the best AI-assisted jobs on the team (see §8.3).

### 6.3 Judging priorities that reshape small-team strategy

Three lines from the judging guidelines change the calculus:

1. **"If teams have equal impact, those who work with FIRST programs rise to the top."** FIRST-program work (starting/mentoring FLL, FTC, FRC teams; hosting/supporting FIRST events) is the **tiebreaker**. Generic community STEM outreach is worth less per hour than FIRST-ecosystem work.
2. **"Watch out for claimed outreach activities that are older. Team success stories should be within the past 2–3 years."** A long history does not help. Recency does.
3. **"Be cautious of hard luck stories. It's ok to have challenges, but the team needs to articulate how they overcame it."** And separately, from the Judge Manual: *"Judges should not try to 'send a message' by recognizing teams that may be from underserved communities."* **Do not run a scarcity narrative.** Run a leverage narrative — what you built with what you had.

Judges are also told to evaluate **sustainability** of each activity and that *"Outreach that is done repeatedly year over year and is successful for the community are still worthy activities."* Repetition is rewarded, novelty is not required.

### 6.4 Minimum viable Impact-competitive outreach program

Derived from the definitions and judging priorities above. This is the smallest program that produces genuinely competitive Impact evidence.

| Commitment | What it satisfies | Realistic cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Start one FLL team** at a local elementary/middle school — help it form, fund or source ≥50% of its registration, support it through its first season, and confirm in writing that they agree you Started them | **Started** — the single highest-value definition, and directly the "works with FIRST programs" tiebreaker | ~$300–600 (FLL registration is far cheaper than FRC) + ~2 students × 2 hrs/week for a season |
| **Mentor one FTC or FRC team** with genuinely weekly contact — video call counts — and get their written agreement | **Mentored** | ~1–2 hrs/week, near-zero cash |
| **Support one FIRST event** — multiple team members volunteering the *entirety* of an event (field reset, scorekeeping, setup/teardown) | **Supported** | 1 weekend, ~4–6 students |
| **Run an Open Alliance build thread + public CAD/GitHub**, and track its view/clone metrics | **Published Resources** with defensible **Reach** | 1–3 hrs/week in build season |
| **One advocacy meeting** — a real meeting with a school board member, city council member, or state legislator's staff about STEM funding or policy (not tabling, not tweeting) | **Advocated** | One afternoon, 2–3 students + a mentor |
| **Two recurring community demos** with a counted, defensible audience (sign-in sheet or organizer letter stating attendance in *your* area) | **Reached**, with documentation | 2 weekends |
| **Documentation form maintained continuously**, with an agreement letter collected at the moment of each activity | Verification of every claim above | ~30 min/week clerical |

**That is roughly 6–8 students and 2 mentors, under $1,000/year, and it hits Started, Mentored, Supported, Published Resources, Advocated and Reached — every category in the definitions document.** A powerhouse team hits the same categories with bigger numbers, but the rubric asks judges to normalize by resources.

The single most common small-team failure is **not collecting the agreement letters at the time of the activity.** Started and Mentored *both* require the other team's affirmative agreement. Retroactively chasing that in February is how teams lose those claims.

## 7. Team Structure

### 7.1 What FIRST actually requires

Very little. Per FIRST's guidance and the [new team criteria](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/new-team-criteria), there is **no FIRST-mandated team structure** and no minimum or maximum student count. FIRST requires adults assigned to the official team contact roles (Lead Coach 1 and 2 have specific Dashboard powers — assigning award submitters, filing submissions). Everything else is your choice.

### 7.2 What a large team looks like

**Team 1678, Citrus Circuits** ([subteams page](https://www.citruscircuits.org/subteams)) runs **six primary subteams plus two secondary**:

- **Business and Media (BaM)** — sponsor contact, grant writing, financial tracking, the annual business plan, social media, newsletters, website, apparel, video
- **Hardware Design** — CAD
- **Hardware Electrical** — the bridge between mechanical and software; crimping, soldering, wiring, power distribution
- **Hardware Fabrication** — mills, lathes, CNC routers; offers **industry-recognized Haas certification**
- **Software Robot** — Java, automation, alignment, motion profiling, path following
- **Software Scouting** — front end (Kotlin app) and back end (Python analysis)
- *Secondary:* **Impact Award** (dedicated subteam for the submission and presentation) and **Strategy** (field playbooks for the drive team)

Leadership: subteam leads + two captains form Student Leadership; Head Coach, Lead Mentor, Captain and Vice Captain form Core Leadership; **subteam leads are appointed by the previous year's Core Leadership.**

Note what this implies: at that scale, the Impact Award has its **own subteam**, and scouting has **two**.

### 7.3 FIRST's own recommendation for smaller teams: the "Specialties" model

This is new and directly on point. [Team Organization Best Practices](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/resources/team-org.pdf), **Rev. Aug. 2026**, introduces "Specialties" as an explicit alternative to subteams:

> *"This approach may be helpful for smaller teams – around 25 students or so."*

The advantages FIRST lists, paraphrased:
- Students can work in several areas instead of being locked into one.
- Roles can change between build season and competition season.
- **Mentors can support students across Specialties without a large mentor-to-subteam ratio.**
- New members find it easier to join a flexible structure than a siloed one.
- Students can show they specialized in several things without moving from subteam to subteam.

Suggested specialties — **Engineering:** Design, Fabrication, Electrical/Pneumatics, Programming, Scouting, Strategy. **Business & Outreach:** Awards, Branding/Media, Financial, Community Engagement.

On leadership, FIRST says Specialties let leaders emerge through example, initiative and skill rather than because a subteam needs a captain.

**This is FIRST explicitly blessing the structure a small team needs.** Adopt the vocabulary in your Impact essay: describing a deliberate Specialty model reads as an intentional organizational choice, not a shortfall.

### 7.4 Minimum viable coverage map

Every function a 60-student team performs, mapped to a ~10–15 student team. The rule: **one owner per function, no exclusivity, and every function has a named backup.**

| Function | Big-team unit | Small-team owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Game analysis & strategy | Strategy subteam | 1 student + lead mentor, whole team in Week 1 | Highest-leverage week of the season |
| CAD / design | Hardware Design | 1–2 students | Onshape; Open Alliance CAD from other teams reduces original design load enormously |
| Fabrication | Hardware Fabrication | 2–3 students | Where in-kind sponsor machining substitutes for capital |
| Electrical | Hardware Electrical | 1–2 students | Must be a *named* owner; the most common single point of failure |
| Robot code | Software Robot | 1–2 students | |
| Scouting | Software Scouting (two subteams at 1678) | 1 student + borrowed/shared open-source scouting app | **Do not build your own scouting app.** Use a published one |
| Drive team | — | 3 students + 1 adult coach | FIRST explicitly supports adult drive coaches (§7.5) |
| Impact Award | Dedicated subteam | 2–3 students, year-round | Must be the *same* students who present |
| Sponsorship & grants | Business and Media | 1–2 students + 1 non-technical mentor | The clearest case for a dedicated non-technical mentor |
| Media / branding | Business and Media | 1 student | Feeds video, social Reach, sponsor deliverables |
| Outreach coordination | Business and Media | 1 student | Owns the documentation form |
| Safety | — | 1 student | |

**Rough floor:** ~10 students and **2–3 mentors**, of whom at least one should be **non-technical** (business/outreach/logistics). Small teams over-index on technical mentors and then have no one who owns money, grants, or the award pipeline — which is exactly where the non-robot deliverables die.

### 7.5 Mentor role — FIRST's official position

This matters for how you frame mentor involvement to judges and to your school. From FIRST Robotics Competition Senior Director Collin Fultz, [The Role of Mentors in FIRST Robotics Competition](https://www.firstinspires.org/robotics/frc/blog/2024-the-role-of-mentors-in-first-robotics-competition):

The question of how involved mentors should be in building the robot comes up every year, and so does the answer: *"However much is needed to inspire the youth on the team."* Fultz is equally direct that FRC is not a contest between robots built by students alone, but a program in which students work with each other and with adult mentors who help them learn and grow.

Fultz also notes mentor involvement will vary year to year with the skills of the current students, that this applies equally to **non-technical** mentoring (*"logistics, business planning, fundraising, and outreach"*), and that there are **no plans to remove adult drive coaches**.

The [2026 Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf) instructs judges accordingly. FIRST's focus is on inspiring young people to become STEM leaders, not on who did the work; students are not required to do all of it, and students and mentors are encouraged to work together:

> *"A team's accomplishments should not be discounted even if you believe that mentors did some or all of the work."*

**However** — note the awards where FIRST *does* impose a student-authorship expectation:
- **Woodie Flowers Finalist Award:** *"it is very important that this be a student-led effort and a student decision. Team students should decide whom to nominate. Team students should write the essay nomination in their own words."* Mentors may check accuracy only.
- **FIRST Leadership Award interview:** conversational, **no presentations or handouts permitted**, and only the nominated student may speak; the mentor observes silently.
- **Impact interview:** only students present; the mentor is a silent observer.

FIRST also has an available [Mentor Network](https://www.firstinspires.org/ways-to-help/volunteer/first-mentor-network) — Fultz cites **more than 1,300 mentors**, technical and non-technical, available to teams regardless of location. For a team short on non-technical mentorship, this is an underused free resource.

## 8. FIRST's Official Position on AI-Generated Content

### 8.1 The policy

This is the current, official, unambiguous stance. It appears **identically** in two places: the [Submitted Awards page](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/submitted-awards) (page updated **July 29, 2026** — i.e., current for the 2027 season) and the [2026 Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf), Ch. 4, under the heading **"Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)"**:

> *"Teams are permitted to use Artificial Intelligence (AI) to assist in the creation of award submissions, handouts, writing robot code, etc."*
>
> *"Teams using AI to assist with code or content generation must provide proper credit and attribution, and respect intellectual property rights and licenses."*

Those are the two sentences that bind a team. The rest of the passage treats AI as a tool like CAD programs and 3D printers, gives `Essay created by Team XXXX and ChatGPT.` as its example of proper credit, tells judges not to rank a team lower for using AI, and says AI-detection sites are unreliable. [`../ai-integration/00_FIRST_AI_POLICY_VERIFIED.md`](../ai-integration/00_FIRST_AI_POLICY_VERIFIED.md) is this project's authority on the policy, and the full Judge Manual text is in `../awards/_text/judge-manual.txt`, which `bash tools/rebuild-corpus.sh` rebuilds locally because FIRST's text is not redistributed.

The Judge Manual adds one point the public page does not: judges may use AI to write award scripts or FIRST Impact Award feedback, provided they credit it and check it for accuracy.

### 8.2 What this actually means — precisely

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| May we use AI on the Impact essay and executive summaries? | **Yes**, explicitly. |
| May we use AI on robot code? | **Yes**, explicitly named. |

### 8.3 Honest assessment: where AI actually helps on these deliverables in 2026

Given the user's goal of using AI to absorb desk work, here is a candid split. This is my assessment, not FIRST policy.

**Genuinely strong — use it here:**
- **Compressing prose to hard character limits.** Fitting a real answer into exactly 500 characters, 13 times, is tedious and mechanical. This is a legitimately excellent use.
- **Normalizing outreach logs into the Documentation Form.** Taking a messy spreadsheet of events and mapping each row to the correct official Definition (Started / Mentored / Supported / Published Resources / Reached / Advocated) with a Documentation ID is pure clerical transformation.
- **Grant boilerplate reuse.** Retargeting the ten standard grant answers (§5.8) to a specific funder's mission language.
- **Interview drilling.** Generating judge-style Q&A from the actual [judging guidelines](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/fia-judging-guidelines.pdf) question list and running mock interviews. High value, zero integrity risk, and it rehearses the students rather than replacing them.
- **Consistency checking.** Cross-checking that numbers in the essay match the exec summaries match the presentation match the documentation form. Judges verify claims; internal contradictions are a real failure mode.
- **Corpus analysis.** Harvesting the published winners (the URL pattern in §2.3) and analyzing structure, density, and topic coverage — as done in this document.

**Weak, risky, or actively harmful:**
- **Writing the essay from a prompt.** The output is generically competent and reads like every other AI-written submission a judge pre-reads that weekend. The winning essays analyzed in §2.3 succeed on a *specific* controlling idea plus dense, verifiable, named specifics — exactly what a model cannot supply without you supplying it first.
- **Any generated number.** This is the serious one. Judges verify claims, and the Reach definition carries an explicit anti-exaggeration clause. A hallucinated or "reasonable-sounding" statistic is a factual misrepresentation in a document FIRST tells judges to check. **Never let a model produce a metric; only let it format metrics you supply.**
- **The Woodie Flowers essay.** FIRST explicitly requires students write this *"in their own words."* Heavy AI drafting here conflicts with the award's stated intent even though the blanket policy permits attributed AI use. Recommend keeping this one human-written.
- **The FIRST Leadership Award interview.** Conversational, no handouts, student speaks alone. Nothing to automate.

### 8.4 Flags for a high-school program

- **FIRST's policy is not your school's policy.** FIRST permits attributed AI use. If the Impact essay is also submitted for course credit, or if your school has a blanket generative-AI prohibition, **the school policy governs that separate use.** Check before the season, not in February.
- **Attribution is a requirement, not a courtesy.** *"must provide proper credit and attribution, and respect intellectual property rights and licenses."* Build it into your submission checklist.
- **The learning is in the interview, not the document.** The submission is pre-read; the award is substantially decided by three students answering questions live for five minutes. Automating the document while *not* investing in student preparation optimizes the wrong artifact. Use the time AI frees to run more mock interviews.
- **Video copyright.** The video spec requires content *"free of copyright restrictions, including music."* AI-generated music and imagery sidestep licensing problems cleanly — a legitimate, low-risk win.

## 9. Small-Team Priority Stack

If you can only do some of this, do it in this order.

1. **Start one FLL team, and get the agreement letter.** Highest-value single action under the definitions, and it is the explicit judging tiebreaker.
2. **Open an Open Alliance build thread on Kickoff day.** It is your engineering notebook, your Published Resources claim, your free design review, and your credibility — one artifact, four uses.
3. **Maintain the Documentation Form continuously from September.** Collect agreement letters at the moment of each activity. This is what converts activity into a verifiable claim.
4. **Get the grant applications in before the November deadlines** — and do not pay registration while a decision is pending.
5. **Use the full 10,000 characters.** Every winner does.
6. **Target Rising All-Star and Team Sustainability deliberately** — under-contested, and structurally favorable to a small team.
7. **Ask a real feedback question** in exec summary #14 and act on the answer within 48 hours.
8. **Recruit one non-technical mentor.** The business/awards pipeline is where small teams lose, and it is the cheapest gap to close.
